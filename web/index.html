Farm machines that pay for an answer before they move.

Five machines on a farm in Kerman, California share one daily budget. Each buys the answer it needs on Telegraph, checks it is about the right place, market and hour, and buys again from another miner when it is not.

Machines are simulated. Answers, payments and receipts are real.

        What an hour of work costs

        Every cent the fleet spends on answers, divided by the work those answers let it do. Holds and unverified calls count too: they are what acting safely costs. Hours per job are simulated; Haul's come from the real drive time it bought.
